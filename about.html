About StreetHazards

Spot a hazard. Report it. Make the street safer.

StreetHazards is a community public-safety platform. Anyone can report road hazards on a live community map, vote when a report is no longer there, and earn points — or play HazardHunt, a timed game that trains your eye to spot dangers in an illustrated city scene. Everything runs in the browser on Firebase; there is no app to install.

Frequently asked questions

1. How do I post a hazard?

Anyone can report — no account needed. Click + Report a hazard, click the map to pin the exact location, pick a type (choose Other to type your own), add optional details, and submit. Guests post under a private device ID; signing in with Google lets you keep your points and reports across devices.

2. Does the map update in real time?

Yes. Reports live in Firestore and every open map listens to changes, so a new report or vote appears for everyone within a moment — no refresh. Your blue location dot comes from your browser's GPS and follows you as you move.

3. I posted a hazard by accident. Can I delete it?

There is no delete button — by design, so one person can't erase another person's report. Instead, anyone can vote Vote: not there anymore on a report; once enough people agree it is marked resolved and disappears from the map for everyone. If you made a genuine mistake, contact the team and an admin can remove it.

4. How do you stop fake posts?

Guests can post so demos stay frictionless, which is an honest trade-off. Two safeguards: the community itself votes stale reports away, and the database write rules are time-boxed to the hackathon window. In production we'd add per-device rate limits and moderation.

5. What is the "Seed demo data" button?

It drops ~50 sample hazards across Washington (clustered around the hackathon venue and Sammamish, then scattered statewide) so the map never looks empty during a demo. The samples are tagged demo and don't affect real reports.
